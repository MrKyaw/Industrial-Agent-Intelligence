# High condenser pressure playbook

High condenser pressure means the chiller cannot reject heat efficiently. Treat a
critical high-pressure alarm as real until the reading is independently verified.

## First checks

1. Confirm the condenser-pressure transmitter against a calibrated reference.
2. Verify condenser-water flow: pump status, valve position, and differential pressure.
3. Check cooling-tower fan operation, drive limits, and air recirculation.
4. Compare condenser approach temperature with the commissioning baseline.

## Likely mechanisms

- **Fouled condenser tubes** raise approach temperature and pressure at stable load.
- **Cooling-tower airflow loss** raises condenser-water temperature before pressure rises.
- **Non-condensable gas** produces a sharp pressure rise with unstable subcooling.
- **Overcharge or control fault** should be ruled out by a qualified refrigeration engineer.

## Safe next actions

Do not reset the high-pressure trip repeatedly. Do not lower the chilled-water
setpoint to compensate. Stabilise load, restore heat rejection, and escalate to a
controls or refrigeration engineer if pressure remains above the alarm limit.
